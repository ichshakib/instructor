## THE UMLAUTS ( উম-লাউটস) : ä, ö, ü

German has three special letters: ä, ö, and ü. They look like normal letters with two dots on top — and those dots change the sound.

| ä | ö | ü |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **das Mädchen**  (girl) (ডাস মেডহেন) | **schön**  (beautiful) (শোন)  | **die Tür**  (door) (ডি ট্যুর)  |

ä, ö, and ü are not decoration — they change the sound of the word.

# Pronouns

## German Personal Pronouns Reference

* **Core Rule**: In German, these words are very important because the verb changes depending on the pronoun.  
* **Sentence Framework**: To build sentences, we need people, which helps us answer who is speaking (ich \[ইহ\]), who we are talking to, and who we are talking about.

| Pronoun | English Translation | Usage Context / Role |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **ich** \[ইহ\] | (I) | Who is speaking |
| **du** \[ডু\] | (you) | Used with friends, family, people you know well |
| **er** \[এয়ার\] | (he) | Who we are talking about |
| **sie** \[জি\] | (she) | Who we are talking about |
| **es** \[এস\] | (it) | Who we are talking about |
| **wir** \[ভিয়ার\] | (we) | Who are we talking to/about |
| **ihr** \[ঈয়া\] | (you all) | Plural informal address |
| **sie** \[জি\] | (they) | Plural subject |
| **Sie** \[জি\] | (formal you) | Used with strangers, in formal or polite situations |

* **Important Distinction & Situations**: Both du \[ডু\] and Sie \[জি\] mean "you", but they are used in completely different situations:

  - **du Situation**: Used exclusively with friends, family, and people you know well.  
  - **Sie Situation**: Used with strangers or in formal and polite situations.

## German Singular Pronouns Reference

* **Core Concept**: Singular pronouns refer to one person or one thing.  
* **Important Reminder**: du \[ডু\] is informal and is specifically used for one person.

| Pronoun | English Meaning | Quick Definition | Example Sentence | Example Translation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **ich** \[ইহ\] | (I) | means I | Ich lerne Deutsch. | I am learning German. |
| **du** \[ডু\] | (you) | means you | Du lernst Deutsch. | You are learning German. |
| **er** \[এয়ার\] | (he) | means he | Er lernt Deutsch. | He is learning German. |
| **sie** \[জি\] | (she) | means she | Sie lernt Deutsch. | She is learning German. |
| **es** \[এস\] | (it) | means it | Es ist gut. | It is good. |

## German Plural Pronouns Reference

* **Core Concept**: Plural pronouns refer to more than one person or one thing. Pronouns replace nouns in a sentence and help us speak clearly and naturally.

| Pronoun | English Meaning | Quick Definition | Rule / Usage Note | Example Sentence | Example Translation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **wir** \[ভিয়ার\] | (we) | means we | Used for a group including yourself | Wir lernen Deutsch. | We are learning German. |
| **ihr** \[ঈয়া\] | (you all / informal plural) | means you all | Used for talking to two or more people in an informal way | Ihr lernt Deutsch. | You are learning German. |
| **sie** \[জি\] | (they) | means they | Can mean she or they; the meaning depends on the sentence | Sie lernen Deutsch. | They are learning German. |
| **Sie** \[জি\] | (you / formal) | means formal you | Capital S \= formal; use with respect | Sie lernen Deutsch. | You are learning German. |

* **Additional Examples & Clarifications**:

  - **sie context**: sie \[জি\] can mean she or they—the meaning depends on the sentence. Examples include:  
    * *Sie ist nett.* (She is nice.)  
    * *Sie sind nett.* (They are nice.)  
  - **Summary Strip**: wir \[ভিয়ার\] \= we • ihr \[ঈয়া\] \= you all • sie \[জি\] \= they • Sie \[জি\] \= formal you

## du vs Sie: Informal and Formal You

* **Core Rule**: Both du \[ডু\] and Sie \[জি\] can mean "you", but they are not used in the same situation. If you are not sure, Sie \[জি\] is usually safer.


| Pronoun | Usage Situations | Example Dialogue | Explanation / Rule |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **du** \[ডু\] *(informal)* | Used with friends, family, children, classmates, or people you know well. | "Hallo, Anna. Wie geht es **dir** \[ডির\]?" (Hello, Anna. How are you?) | Here, we use du \[ডু\] because Anna is someone familiar. |
| **Sie** \[জি\] *(formal)* | Used with a teacher, a doctor, a stranger, or in a shop. | "Guten Tag. Wie heißen **Sie** \[জি\]?" *(Good day. What is your name?)* | Here, Sie \[জি\] is polite and formal. |

* **Cultural Note & Practice**:

  - **Cultural note**: Later, people may say: *Wir können du \[ডু\] sagen.* (We can use du \[ডু\].)  
  - **Practice advice**: Practice both and listen to how native speakers choose between du \[ডু\] and Sie \[জি\].

# The Verb sein (to be) Reference

* **English Equivalents**: I am, you are, he is, she is, we are, they are.  
* **Core Concept**: Now we use the personal pronouns with one of the most important verbs in German: **sein \[জাইন\] (to be)**. Let’s learn it slowly, clearly, and with simple examples.

| Use Case for sein \[জাইন\] | Category Description | Example Sentence & Translation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Names** | Who we are | *Ich bin Anna.* \[ইখ বিন আনা\] (I am Anna) |
| **Nationality** | Where we come from | *Ich bin aus Deutschland.* \[ইহ বিন আউস ডয়েচল্যান্ড\] (I am from Germany) |
| **Jobs** | What we do | *Ich bin Student.* \[ইহ বিন স্টুডেন্ট\] (I am a student) |
| **Feelings** | How we feel | *Ich bin müde.* \[ইহ বিন মিউডে\] (I am tired) |
| **Age** | How old we are | *Ich bin zwanzig Jahre alt.* \[ইহ বিন জোবানজিশ ইয়ারে আল্ট\] (I am twenty years old) |
| **Simple descriptions** | Facts about us or things | *Das ist gut.* \[দাস ইষ্ট গুট\] (That is good) |

* **Core Concept**: With ich, du, er, sie, es, these are the singular forms. Let’s start with the basic forms of *sein* \[জাইন\].  
* **Quick Summary Strip**: Remember: ich bin \[ইখ বিন\], du bist \[ডু বিষ্ট\], er ist \[এয়ার ইষ্ট\], sie ist \[জি ইষ্ট\], es ist \[এস ইষ্ট\].

| Pronoun \+ Conjugation | English Meaning | Example Sentence | Example Translation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

| **du bist** \[ডু বিষ্ট\] | (you are) | Du bist nett. \[ডু বিষ্ট নেট\] | You are nice |
| **er ist** \[এয়ার ইষ্ট\] | (he is) | Er ist müde. \[এয়ার ইষ্ট মুডে\] | He is tired |

* **Quick Summary Strip**: Remember: wir sind \[ভিয়ার জিনড\], ihr seid \[ঈয়া জেইড\], sie sind \[জি জিনড\], Sie sind \[জি জিনড\].

* **Core Concept**: Use *sein* \[জাইন\] to say who you are, what you do, where you're from, and how old you are (focusing on names, jobs, nationality, and age).

# The Verb haben (to have) Reference

* **Core Concept**: Now that we know the verb *sein* (to be), we learn another very important German verb: **haben \[হাবেন\] \= to have**.  
* **English Equivalents**: In English we say: I have, you have, he has, she has, we have, they have.  
* **Goal**: Today we will learn *haben* \[হাবেন\] with German personal pronouns.

| Usage Category for haben \[হাবেন\] | Description / Details | Example Sentence & Translation | Pronunciation Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Things** | Used for physical objects | *Ich habe ein Buch.* \[ইখ হবে আইন বুখ\] (I have a book.) | *haben* \[হাবেন\] is not only about objects. |
| **Family** | Used for relations | *Ich habe eine Familie.* \[ইখ হবে আইন ফামিলিয়ে\] (I have a family.) | Used to describe family members and personal relationships. |
| **Time** | Used for schedules/time | *Ich habe Zeit.* \[ইখ হবে জাইট\] (I have time.) | Used when talking about having or lacking free time. |
| **Hunger** | Used for physical states (In German: I have hunger) | *Ich habe Hunger.* \[ইখ হবে হুঙ্গার\] (I am hungry.) | Demonstrates *haben* \[হাবেন\] in everyday states. |
| **Thirst** | Used for physical states | *Ich habe Durst.* \[ইখ হবে ডুরষ্ট\] (I am thirsty.) | Used to express feeling thirsty in daily conversation. |
| **Everyday Situations** | Used in various common scenarios | *Ich habe Fragen.* \[ইখ হবে ফ্রাগেন\] (I have questions.) | In German, *haben* \[হাবেন\] is used very often. |

* **Quick Summary Strip**: Remember: ich habe \[ইখ হবে\], du hast \[ডু হাষ্ট\], er hat \[এয়ার হাট\], sie hat \[জি হাট\], es hat \[এস হাট\].

| Pronoun \+ Conjugation | English Meaning | Example Sentence | Example Translation | Pronunciation Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **ich habe** \[ইখ হবে\] | (I have) | Ich habe ein Buch. \[ইখ হবে আইন বুখ\] | I have a book. | Used with the first-person singular pronoun. |
| **du hast** \[ডু হাষ্ট\] | (you have) | Du hast Zeit. \[ডু হাষ্ট জাইট\] | You have time. | Used with the informal singular "you" pronoun. |
| **er hat** \[এয়ার হাট\] | (he has) | Er hat Hunger. \[এয়ার হাট হুঙ্গার\] | He is hungry. (He has hunger) | Used with the masculine singular pronoun. |

| **es hat** \[এস হাট\] | (it has) | Es hat einen Namen. \[এস হাট আইনেন নামেন\] | It has a name. | Used with the neuter singular pronoun. |

**Comprehensive Guide to German Yes/No Questions**

**Core Rule and Structure**  
In German, forming a yes/no question requires placing the conjugated verb first in the sentence, rather than in the second position used for standard statements. The structural pattern follows:

$$\\text{Verb} \+ \\text{subject} \+ \\text{rest}$$  
These questions are answered using **Ja** (Yes) or **Nein** (No).

**1\. Questions with "sein" (to be)**  
When using the irregular verb *sein*, invert the subject and conjugated form to transform a statement into a question.

| Statement (Verb Second) | Question (Verb First) | English Translation | Pronunciation Guide |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Du bist müde.** | **Bist du müde?** | Are you tired? | BIST doo MOO-duh? |
| **Er ist krank.** | **Ist er krank?** | Is he sick? | IST air KRAHNK? |
| **Sie ist Lehrerin.** | **Ist sie Lehrerin?** | Is she a teacher? | IST zee LEH-ruh-rin? |
| **Wir sind bereit.** | **Sind wir bereit?** | Are we ready? | ZINT veer buh-RIGHT? |

* **Sein Question Pattern Recap:** *Bist du ...?*, *Ist er ...?*, *Ist sie ...?*, *Sind wir ...?*  
* 

**2\. Questions with "haben" (to have)**  
The verb *haben* follows the exact same inversion mechanism.

| Statement | Question | English Translation | Pronunciation Guide |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Du hast Zeit.** | **Hast du Zeit?** | Do you have time? | HAHST doo TSIGHT? |
| **Er hat Hunger.** | **Hat er Hunger?** | Is he hungry? | HAHT air HOONG-er? |
| **Sie hat Durst.** | **Hat sie Durst?** | Is she thirsty? | HAHT zee DOORST? |
| **Sie haben einen Termin.** | **Haben Sie einen Termin?** (Formal) | Do you have an appointment? | HAH-ben zee AI-nen tair-MEEN? |

**3\. Regular Verb Questions**  
Regular verbs adopt the identical verb-first structure when converting a declarative statement into an inquiry.

| Statement | Question | English Translation | Pronunciation Guide |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Du lernst Deutsch.** | **Lernst du Deutsch?** | Are you learning German? | LAIRNST doo DOYTCH? |

**Comprehensive Guide to German W-Questions (W-Fragen)**

**Core Rule and Structure**  
Unlike yes/no questions that seek confirmation, W-questions ask for specific information and cannot be answered with just *Ja* or *Nein*. The structural pattern places the interrogative word first, followed immediately by the conjugated verb:

$$\\text{W-word} \+ \\text{verb} \+ \\text{subject} \+ \\text{rest}$$  
**1\. Wer & Was (Who & What)**

* **wer** \= who (used for people)  
*   
* **was** \= what (used for things and actions)  
* 

| Example Sentence | English Translation | Usage Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Wer bist du?** | Who are you? | Asking about a person. |
| **Wer ist das?** / **Das ist Anna.** | Who is that? / That is Anna. | Identifying someone. |
| **Was machst du?** / **Ich lerne Deutsch.** | What are you doing? / I am learning German. | Asking about an action. |
| **Was ist das?** / **Das ist ein Buch.** | What is that? / That is a book. | Identifying a thing. |

**2\. Wo, Woher & Wohin (Location & Direction)**

* **wo** \= where (location)  
*   
* **woher** \= where from (origin)  
*   
* **wohin** \= where to (direction/destination)  
* 

| Example Sentence | English Translation | Usage Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Wo wohnst du?** / **Ich wohne in Kairo.** | Where do you live? / I live in Cairo. | Fixed location. |
| **Wo ist der Bahnhof?** | Where is the train station? | Finding a place. |
| **Woher kommst du?** / **Ich komme aus Ägypten.** | Where are you from? / I come from Egypt. | Origin. |
| **Wohin gehst du?** / **Ich gehe nach Hause.** | Where are you going? / I am going home. | Destination/Direction. |

**3\. Wann, Wie & Warum (Time, Manner & Reason)**

* **wann** \= when (time)  
*   
* **wie** \= how (manner, name, health)  
*   
* **warum** \= why (reason)  
* 

| Example Sentence | English Translation | Usage Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Wann arbeitest du?** / **Ich arbeite morgen.** | When do you work? / I work tomorrow. | Time inquiry. |
| **Wie geht es dir?** / **Mir geht es gut.** | How are you? / I am fine. | Wellbeing. |
| **Wie heißt du?** / **Ich heiße Omar.** | What is your name? / My name is Omar. | Naming (literally "How are you called?"). |
| **Warum lernst du Deutsch?** / **Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Deutschland arbeiten möchte.** | Why are you learning German? / I am learning German because I want to work in Germany. | Reason (*weil* sends the verb to the end). |

**Quick Summary of W-Words**

* **Wer:** People  
*   
* **Was:** Things & actions  
*   
* **Wo / Woher / Wohin:** Places, origin, and direction  
*   
* **Wann:** Time  
*   
* **Wie:** Manner, names, and conditions  
*   
* **Warum:** Reasons

**Akkusativ & Adjectives Study Guide (Deutsch A1)**

**Core Concepts: Nominativ vs. Akkusativ**

* **Nominativ \= Subject:** The subject does the action and answers the question *Who?* (**wer**) for people or *What?* (**was**) for things.  
*   
* **Akkusativ \= Direct Object:** The direct object receives the action of the verb and answers the question *Whom?* (**wen**) for people or *What?* (**was**) for things.  
* 

**Question Words for Finding Objects:**

* **wer?** (*vair*) – Who? (Used to find the subject for people)  
*   
* **wen?** (*ven*) – Whom? / Who? (Used to find the direct object when it is a person)  
*   
* **was?** (*vahs*) – What? (Used to find the direct object when it is a thing, food, drink, or an idea)  
* 

**Examples of Subject and Direct Object in Sentences:**

* **Der Mann liest ein Buch.** (*dair mahn leest yn bookh* – The man is reading a book.)  
* 

  * *Der Mann* \= Subject (Nominativ)  
  *   
  * *ein Buch* \= Direct Object (Akkusativ, thing)  
  *   
  * *Question:* **Was liest er?** (*vahs leest air* – What is he reading?) \-\> **ein Buch**  
  *

**Comprehensive German Study Notes: Akkusativ Articles & Masculine Changes**

**1\. Core Principles of the Akkusativ Case**

* **Akkusativ \= Direct Object: The Akkusativ case is used for the direct object of a sentence, which is the person or thing that receives the action of the verb.**  
*   
* **Nominativ \= Subject: The subject performs the action and answers the question *Who?* (wer?) or *What?* (was?).**  
*   
* **The Golden Rule for Masculine Articles: In the Akkusativ case, masculine articles change clearly and distinctively, while feminine and neuter articles stay mostly the same in this basic A1 pattern.**  
* 

**2\. Masculine Article Transformations (Nominativ to Akkusativ)**

**When a masculine noun transitions from the subject (Nominativ) to the direct object (Akkusativ), the article undergoes a predictable shift by adding an \-en ending:**

* **Definite Article: der becomes den (*dair* to *dayn*)**  
*   
* **Indefinite Article: ein becomes einen (*yn* to *IY-nen*)**  
*   
* **Negative Article: kein becomes keinen (*kyn* to *KY-nen*)**  
*   
* **Possessive Article: mein becomes meinen (*myn* to *MY-nen*)**  
* 

**3\. Detailed Breakdown and Examples**

**Definite Article Shifts (*der* to *den*)**

* **Nominativ Example: Der Mann ist hier. (*dair mahn ist heer* – The man is here.)**  
*   
* **Akkusativ Example: Ich sehe den Mann. (*ikh zeh-heh dayn mahn* – I see the man.)**  
*   
* **Question Formulation: Wen sehe ich? (*ven zeh-heh ikh* – Who do I see?) \-\> Answer: den Mann**  
*   
* **Additional Example: Ich nehme den Bus. (*ikh NEH-meh dayn boos* – I take the bus.)**  
* 

**Indefinite Article Shifts (*ein* to *einen*)**

* **Nominativ Example: Ein Mann ist hier. (*yn mahn ist heer* – A man is here.)**  
*   
* **Akkusativ Example: Ich sehe einen Mann. (*ikh zeh-heh IY-nen mahn* – I see a man.)**  
*   
* **Question Formulation: Wen sehe ich? (*ven zeh-heh ikh* – Who do I see?) \-\> Answer: einen Mann**  
*   
* **Additional Examples:**  
* 

  * **Ich trinke einen Kaffee. (*ikh TRINK-eh IY-nen KAH-fay* – I am drinking a coffee.)**  
  *   
  * **Ich nehme einen Bus. (*ikh NEH-meh IY-nen boos* – I take a bus.)**  
  * 

**Negative Article Shifts (*kein* to *keinen*)**

* **Meaning: kein means "not a" or "no" (*kyn* \= *not a / no*).**  
*   
* **Nominativ Example: Das ist kein Mann. (*dahs ist kyn mahn* – That is not a man.)**  
*   
* **Akkusativ Example: Ich sehe keinen Mann. (*ikh zeh-heh KY-nen mahn* – I see no man / I do not see a man.)**  
*   
* **Extra Sentences:**

  *   
  * **Ich habe keinen Stift. (*ikh HAH-beh KY-nen stift* – I do not have a pen.)**  
  * 

**Possessive Article Shifts (*mein* to *meinen*)**

* **Meaning: mein means "my" (*myn* \= *my*).**  
*   
* **Nominativ Example: Mein Vater ist hier. (*myn FAH-ter ist heer* – My father is here.)**  
*   
* **Akkusativ Example: Ich sehe meinen Vater. (*ikh zeh-heh MY-nen FAH-ter* – I see my father.)**  
*   
* **Extra Sentences:**  
* 

  * **Ich treffe meinen Bruder. (*ikh TREF-eh MY-nen BROO-der* – I meet my brother.)**  
  *   
  * **Ich nehme meinen Bus. (*ikh NEH-meh MY-nen boos* – I take my bus.)**

**Comprehensive Study Notes: Negation in German (nicht vs. kein)**

**1\. Core Concepts of Negation**

* **Overview of Negation: German primarily uses two words for negation depending on the context: nicht (*nikht* – not) and kein / keine (*kyn / KY-neh* – not a / no).**  
*   
* **The Golden Rule: Nicht negates verbs, adjectives, and full sentence ideas, whereas kein / keine negates nouns that have an indefinite article or no article.**  
* 

**2\. Negation with kein / keine**

* **Definition: kein / keine means "not a" or "no".**  
*   
* **Grammatical Relationship: kein is the negative form of the indefinite masculine/neuter article ein, while keine is the negative form of the feminine article eine.**  
* 

**Examples of Noun Negation:**

* **Ich habe ein Buch. (*ikh HAH-beh yn bookh* – I have a book.)**

* 

**3\. Negation with nicht**

**nicht (*nikht* – not) is used across three main grammatical contexts: with verbs, with adjectives, and with full sentence ideas.**

**Category A: Negating Verbs and Actions**

* **Ich komme. (*ikh KOH-meh* – I am coming.)**

*   
* **Ich lerne heute nicht. (*ikh LEAR-neh HOY-teh nikht* – I am not learning today.)**  
*

**Common Dative Prepositions**

In German, certain prepositions are always followed by the dative case.

* **mit** \[mit\] means "with". It is used with people or means of transport (changing masculine **der Bus** to **dem Bus**, and neuter **das Auto** to **dem Auto**).  
* 

  * **Ich lerne mit Omar.** \[ikh lair-nuh mit oh-mar\] \= I learn with Omar.  
  *   
  * **Ich trinke Kaffee mit Anna.** \[ikh trin-kuh kaf-fay mit an-nah\] \= I drink coffee with Anna.  
  *   
  * **Ich fahre mit dem Bus.** \[ikh fah-ruh mit dame boos\] \= I go by bus.  
  *   
  * **Ich fahre mit dem Auto.** \[ikh fah-ruh mit dame ow-toh\] \= I go by car.  
  *   
* **bei** \[by\] means "at" or "with" (referring to a person's place or location). When combined with the article **dem**, it contracts to **beim** \[byme\].  
* 

  * **Ich bin bei Anna.** \[ikh bin by an-nah\] \= I am at Anna's place / I am with Anna.  
  *   
  * **Ich bin bei dem Arzt** (or **beim Arzt**) \[ikh bin by dame ahrtst / byme ahrtst\] \= I am at the doctor.  
  *   
* **von** \[fon\] means "from" or "of". It is used for possession or coming from a person/place. The feminine article **die Arbeit** changes to **der Arbeit**.  
* 

  * **Das Geschenk ist von Anna.** \[daas ge-shenk ist fon an-nah\] \= The gift is from Anna.  
  *   
  * **Ich komme von der Arbeit.** \[ikh kom-uh fon dair ar-byte\] \= I come from work.  
  *   
* **nach** \[nakh\] means "to" or "after". It is used for cities, countries, and home (e.g., **nach Berlin**, **nach Deutschland**, **nach Hause**).  
*   
* **aus** \[owss\] means "from" or "out of". It is used for origin, countries, cities, or coming out of a building.  
* 

  * **Ich komme aus Ägypten.** \[ikh kom-uh owss e-gyp-ten\] \= I come from Egypt / I am from Egypt.  
  *   
  * **Ich komme aus Deutschland.** \[ikh kom-uh owss doytch-land\] \= I am from Germany.  
  *   
  * **Ich komme aus Kairo.** \[ikh kom-uh owss ky-roh\] \= I come from Cairo.  
  *   
  * **Ich komme aus der Schule.** \[ikh kom-uh owss dair shoo-luh\] \= I come from school.  
  *   
* **zu** \[tsoo\] means "to". It is used for a person, place, or appointment. It contracts with articles: **zu dem** becomes **zum**, and **zu der** becomes **zur**.  
* 

  * **Ich gehe zu Anna.** \[ikh gay-huh tsoo an-nah\] \= I am going to Anna.  
  *

**Comprehensive Study Notes: German Modal Verbs (Modalverben)**

**Overview of Modal Verbs**

* Modal verbs help express ideas like can, must, want, would like, may, and should.  
*   
* The six important modal verbs in German are **können** \[kœ-nen\] (can), **müssen** \[mues-sen\] (must), **wollen** \[vol-len\] (want), **möchten** \[mœkh-ten\] (would like), **dürfen** \[duer-fen\] (may / allowed to), and **sollen** \[zol-len\] (should).  
*   
* Modal verbs usually work with another verb in the sentence.  
*   
* Key sentence structure rule: In a simple sentence, the modal verb is in position 2, and the second verb goes to the end in its infinitive form. The modal verb comes early and the other verb comes last.  
*   
* Example: **Ich kann Deutsch sprechen.** \[ikh kan doytsh shprekh-en\] (I can speak German.)  
*   
* Example: **Ich muss heute lernen.** \[ikh moos hoy-tuh lair-nen\] (I must study today.)  
* 

**1\. können (Ability, Skill, or Possibility)**

* Meaning: **können** \[kœ-nen\] means **can** or **to be able to**.  
*   
* Usage: It is used to express ability, skill, or possibility, or to talk about what you or others are able to do.  
*   
* Important Forms (Conjugation):  
* 

  * **ich kann** \[ikh kan\] \- I can  
  *   
  * **du kannst** \[doo kanst\] \- you can  
  *   
  * **er kann** \[air kan\] \- he can  
  *   
  * **sie kann** \[zee kan\] \- she can  
  *   
  * **wir können** \[veer kœ-nen\] \- we can  
  *   
* Examples:  
* 

  * **Ich kann Deutsch sprechen.** \[ikh kan doytsh shprekh-en\] \- I can speak German.  
  *   
  * **Ich kann gut schwimmen.** \[ikh kan goot shvim-men\] \- I can swim well.  
  *   
  * **Du kannst gut kochen.** \[doo kanst goot kokh-en\] \- You can cook well.  
  *   
  * **Wir können heute kommen.** \[veer kœ-nen hoy-tuh kom-men\] \- We can come today.  
  * 

**2\. müssen (Necessity)**

* Meaning: **müssen** \[mues-sen\] means **must** or **have to**.  
*   
* Usage: We use it when something is necessary, important, or required.  
*   
* Important Forms (Conjugation):  
* 

  * **ich muss** \[ikh moos\] \- I must  
  *   
  * **du musst** \[doo moost\] \- you must  
  *   
  * **er muss** \[air moos\] \- he must  
  *   
  * **sie muss** \[zee moos\] \- she must  
  *   
  * **wir müssen** \[veer mues-sen\] \- we must  
  *   
* Examples:  
* 

  * **Ich muss lernen.** \[ikh moos lair-nen\] \- I must study.  
  *   
  * **Ich muss arbeiten.** \[ikh moos ar-by-ten\] \- I have to work.  
  *   
  * **Ich muss heute lernen.** \[ikh moos hoy-tuh lair-nen\] \- I must study today.  
  *   
  * **Du musst früh kommen.** \[doo moost frueh kom-en\] \- You must come early.  
  *   
  * **Wir müssen Deutsch lernen.** \[veer mues-sen doytsh lair-nen\] \- We have to learn German.  
  * 

**3\. wollen (Strong Wish or Plan)**

* Meaning: **wollen** \[vol-len\] means **to want**.  
*   
* Usage: It is used for a strong wish, a plan, or something someone really wants to do.  
*   
* Important Forms (Conjugation):  
* 

  * **ich will** \[ikh vil\] \- I want  
  *   
  * **du willst** \[doo vilst\] \- you want  
  *   
  * **er will** \[air vil\] \- he wants  
  *   
  * **sie will** \[zee vil\] \- she wants  
  *   
  * **wir wollen** \[veer vol-len\] \- we want  
  *   
* Examples:  
* 

  * **Ich will Kaffee trinken.** \[ikh vil kaf-fay trin-ken\] \- I want to drink coffee.  
  *   
  * **Ich will nach Berlin fahren.** \[ikh vil nakh bair-leen fah-ren\] \- I want to go to Berlin.  
  *   
  * **Du willst Deutsch lernen.** \[doo vilst doytsh lair-nen\] \- You want to learn German.  
  *   
  * **Wir wollen Pizza essen.** \[veer vol-len pit-tsah es-sen\] \- We want to eat pizza.  
  * 

**4\. möchten (Polite Wish)**

* Meaning: **möchten** \[mœkh-ten\] means **would like**.  
*   
* Usage: It is softer and more polite than **wollen** \[vol-len\]. It is very common in shops, restaurants, classes, and everyday conversations. We use **möchten** \[mœkh-ten\] when we want to be polite or make a kind request. Using **möchten** \[mœkh-ten\] shows good manners (Höflich bitten kommt gut an\!) and makes your German sound natural.  
*   
* Important Forms (Conjugation):  
*

  * **du möchtest** \[doo mœkh-test\] \- you would like  
  *

  *   
  * **sie möchte** \[zee mœkh-tuh\] \- she would like  
  *   
  * **wir möchten** \[veer mœkh-ten\] \- we would like  
  *   
* Examples:

**6\. sollen (Advice, Recommendation, or Task)**

* Meaning: **sollen** \[zol-len\] means **should** or **be supposed to**.

### **Part 2: The German Imperative (*Der Imperativ*)**

The imperative mood is used to tell or ask someone to do something. German has three primary forms depending on who you are addressing, and adding the word **bitte** (\[BIT-tuh\] \- please) makes commands softer and more polite.

* **Target Audience:** Use this form for one familiar person, such as a friend, a classmate, a child, or someone you know well.

**Grammar Focus: Separable Verbs (Trennbare Verben)**

* **The Structural Rule**: In German simple main clauses, the conjugated verb base occupies position two, while the separable prefix detaches entirely and moves to the very end of the sentence.  
*   
* **Structure Model**: Subject \+ Conjugated Verb Part (Position Two) \+ Object/Adverbial \+ Separable Prefix (End).  
*   
* **Example Breakdown**:  
* 

  * Full verb: *einkaufen* \[ine-kow-fen\] (to shop) transforms in a sentence to **Ich kaufe heute ein.** \[ikh kow-fuh hoy-tuh ine\] (I go shopping today.)  
  *   
  * Full verb: *anrufen* \[ahn-roo-fen\] (to call) transforms in a sentence to **Ich rufe Anna an.** \[ikh roof-uh ah-nah ahn\] (I call Anna.)  
  *

### **2\. The Accusative Case (Akkusativ) in the Supermarket**

When you perform an action like buying (*kaufen*), taking (*nehmen*), or wanting (*möchten*), the direct object receives the action and shifts into the **Akkusativ** case. In German, only masculine nouns change their article in the accusative case (changing from *der* to *den*, and *ein* to *einen*). Feminine and neuter articles remain unchanged.

**Grammar & Linguistic Notes**

* **Accusative Case with Searching:** When using **Ich suche** ("I am looking for"), masculine nouns change their article from *der* to *den* (e.g., *den Bahnhof*), while feminine (*die*) and neuter (*das*) articles remain unchanged.  
*   
* **Transport Preposition:** Means of transport use the preposition **mit** followed by the dative case (*mit dem Bus*, *mit der Bahn*, *mit dem Zug*).  
*   
* **Separable Verbs:** Verbs like **abbiegen** (to turn) and **umsteigen** (to change transport) split in conjugated sentences, sending the prefix (*ab-* or *oom-*) to the very end of the clause (e.g., *Biegen Sie links ab.*).  
*   
* **Expressing Pain:** Body parts coupled with **tut weh** provide an immediate structure for physical discomfort (e.g., *Mein Kopf tut weh.*).
